# Player evaluation plan

Owner: Project Lead. Programme: [four pillars](improvement-plan.md).
Status: proposed on 5 November 2026; no participant results collected.

## Questions that decide the next work

1. Can a new player predict what an action spends now and what a tide spends later?
2. Can they predict drift, shelter and whether stress permits growth or victory?
3. Does a tide explain its causes without requiring a guide?
4. Can they recover, resume and find the next map without losing progress?
5. Do later maps invite different plans, and is a second attempt appealing?
6. Do art and sound support decisions while making the sea enjoyable to inhabit?

## First round: first-time comprehension

Use 3–5 willing participants unfamiliar with the game for an exploratory round.
Give each a fresh, isolated browser profile on the same recorded build. Record
viewport, browser, build, settings and prior strategy-game familiarity. No recruitment
or external messaging is implied by this plan.

Ask the participant to think aloud and play First Light. Avoid suggesting actions
until they request help or cannot continue. Record assistance so aided success is
distinguishable from independent comprehension.

At natural pauses, ask neutral prediction questions:

| Moment | Prompt | Record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before nourishment | What do you expect this action to do now and later? | Immediate cost versus queued growth |
| Before an early advance | What do you expect to change when the tide ends? | Drift, weather, recovery, production, growth and rations |
| Before a storm | Which bells will be safe, and why? | Post-drift versus current geometry; cues used |
| After stress changes | What would make this island recover or grow? | Recovery rate and safe conditions |
| Before tide 8 | What must still be true to succeed? | Four bell conditions and Heart survival |
| After a loss or warning | What would you change next time? | Actionable diagnosis; discovery of replanning |
| After finishing | What would make you want to continue? | Next-map curiosity, medals, scenery, challenge or none |

Mark where a question itself directed attention; do not count that as unaided learning.

## Second round: campaign variety and sensory pacing

Use returning players after first-voyage problems are addressed. Sample maps 4, 6 and
8 for resource pressure and fleet complexity, retaining full eight-map automated and
browser coverage for release. Use designated evaluation profiles for unlocked maps;
never rewrite a participant's personal save.

Record chosen strategies and reasons, plans abandoned after using forecast, action
choices, unused actions, retries, and changes between attempts. A quiet tide is not
automatically a defect: distinguish planning, satisfying safety, confusion and bored
waiting. Different command order alone is not a different strategy.

Ask whether maps feel distinct, the next objective feels worthwhile, and medals
communicate a desirable challenge. Observe the tide sequence before asking about
pace; record Skip use and its reason. Include a full-campaign listening pass for cue
audibility, transitions, repetition and fatigue. Muted play must remain understandable.

## Measures and decisions

- Record prediction accuracy by concept, assistance, hesitation, accidental actions,
  misunderstood outcomes, and ability to explain a retry plan.
- Check selection, forecast, shelter and danger at 1366×768 and 1920×1080, alongside
  keyboard operation, non-colour cues and reduced motion.
- Use repeated misunderstandings to prioritize small teaching or presentation changes.
  Treat isolated observations as hypotheses, not universal claims.
- Before changing difficulty, reproduce the problem, check alternative routes, and
  specify the player choice the change improves. Retest that concept afterward.
- Report counts with denominators and context. This qualitative round does not
  establish population completion rates or prove that the game is fun.

## Report template

Build/profile and viewport; participant code and experience; task; predicted versus
observed behaviour; assistance; participant explanation; severity; affected pillar;
smallest proposed change; acceptance criterion; remaining uncertainty.

Project Lead merges findings into `docs/todo.md`, assigns exclusive file ownership,
and requires simulation, persistence and browser regression checks for changes.
